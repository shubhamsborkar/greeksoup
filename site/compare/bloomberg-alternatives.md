## What a terminal is actually used for

An equity analyst uses a terminal for five jobs. The first is quotes and charts. The second is the company's own record, meaning its filings, its statements and its results dates. The third is who owns and trades the stock, which is fund holdings, insider trades, short interest and the options tape. The fourth is the market around it, so rates, currencies, commodities and the macro prints. The fifth is the desk itself, the book, the risk on it and the notes that build up over years. The free tools below each cover some of those jobs. None of them covers the news wire, the chat, or the sell-side research a terminal bundles in, and it is worth knowing that before the search begins.

## The free tools, one at a time

Everything below is as we found it in September 2026. Free tiers change; check the vendor's own page before deciding.

**Yahoo Finance.** Quotes, charts, a profile, the headline financials, analyst counts and the insider table, on every listed company in the world, free, in a browser. It is the widest free record there is and the feed most free tools, GreekSoup included, draw quotes from. What it lacks is any desk: no book across markets, no 13F tracker, no notes, and the site is built around ads and a paid tier.

**TradingView.** The best free charting on the web, with a large library of indicators and a community that publishes scripts and ideas. The free plan carries ads and allows two indicators on a chart. It is a charting tool first; the fundamental record is thin next to the others here.

**Finviz.** A fast screener and heat map for US stocks, free with quotes delayed fifteen to twenty minutes, plus a compact fundamentals table with three years of financials and insider trades per name. The free tier is US-only in practice and the pages carry ads.

**Stock Analysis.** Clean statements, ratios and a screener, about five years of financials free on US names and a growing set of other markets, with longer history on the paid plan. Good for reading a company's numbers on one page. There is no portfolio, no ownership data beyond the headline holders, and no macro.

**Koyfin.** The closest free thing to a terminal screen. The free plan gives charting, market and macro dashboards across many countries, a global screener, one portfolio, two watchlists, two screens, two years of financials and one year of estimates, with delayed data. Filings, transcripts and deeper history sit behind the paid plans, which start at thirty-nine dollars a month.

**Perplexity Finance.** An AI answer engine with a finance hub: quotes and charts, an earnings hub with transcripts, a screener in plain English that covers US and Indian names, and ownership views for holders, insiders and politicians. The hub is largely free; the model picker, file uploads and portfolio tracking assume the twenty-dollar plan. It answers from the web and its data partners rather than from a screen you control. [GreekSoup vs Perplexity](/docs/compare/vs-perplexity/) goes into that difference.

**OpenBB.** The open-source terminal that began as Gamestonk Terminal in December 2020 and grew into a Python data platform plus a web workspace. On 25 August 2026 its founder announced the company is winding down and releasing the entire product suite under a permissive licence. What that leaves is a large and capable codebase for people comfortable in Python or Docker, with an uncertain maintainer. [The open-source field](/docs/compare/open-source-equity-research-software/) has more.

**GreekSoup.** Free and open source under the MIT licence. Sixteen screens on your own computer: your broker book, a hand-kept book in any market, watchlists, risk, the 13F tracker from SEC EDGAR, the options tape from CBOE, FINRA short interest, Congress trades, a calendar of every dated event on your names, twenty-two FRED series, fifty-four commodities with the industries each moves, your own value-chain maps, and a research vault of plain files. Fifteen of the sixteen screens run with no key at all. An Ask box on every screen sends that screen's numbers to whichever AI you already use. One pasted line installs it on a Mac, Windows or Linux; no coding.

## Where GreekSoup is the wrong answer

- **Live level-two quotes and a professional chart.** The desk prices from Yahoo's free feed and your broker. TradingView or your broker's own platform is the better chart.
- **Transcripts and the news wire.** The ticker page shows headlines with a feed key. It does not carry call transcripts; Perplexity's earnings hub and Koyfin's paid plans do.
- **Sell-side estimates and deep statement history without a key.** Six years of statements, ratio history, estimates and peers need a data provider key. Stock Analysis and Koyfin show a few years free in the browser with no setup.
- **Screening the whole market.** There is no screener. Finviz, Koyfin and Stock Analysis each have one.
- **Anything on a phone or on someone else's computer.** The desk answers only on the computer it runs on, by design, so it needs no login.

## Pick by the job

If you want a chart, TradingView is the one. If you want a company's numbers on one page, Stock Analysis or Koyfin. If the question is about today, Perplexity.
